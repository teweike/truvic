WELDING · AUTOMATION

Automatic Tank Welding Center

A tank shell welding cell assembled from three parts that have to work as one: a welding manipulator, a self-aligning welding rotator, and a submerged arc welding package. The vessel sits on the rotator, which turns it at a set speed; the manipulator brings the head to the seam and travels along it. The boom moves 4000 mm vertically and 4000 mm horizontally, carries 100 kg at the end, and the column rotates under power to place the head between seams. The rotator takes 5 t and covers 250 to 2300 mm diameter, adjusting its rollers self-aligningly so a change of vessel size does not need a new setting.

Machine in detail

The cell in a tank shop, and the seams it leaves.

Key Advantages

One cell instead of a positioner, a rotator and a separate column.

Both seam directions from one setup

The rotator turns the vessel for circumferential seams and the boom travels for longitudinal seams, without moving the vessel off the rollers.

Self-aligning rollers

The roller set aligns itself to the vessel diameter, so a change from one tank size to another is a change of setting, not a change of base.

Submerged arc deposition

A 1000 A SAW package with flux recovery deposits a full seam in one or two passes and reuses the flux.

Head placed, then inched

Lift at 1000 mm/min positions the head, the 120–1000 mm/min stretch and the 100 x 100 mm slide trim it onto the seam.

Operator at the pendant

Positioning and welding are started from the remote pendant, so the operator watches the arc rather than the column.

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions buyers ask before a tank welding cell is specified.

What vessel sizes does it cover?

250 to 2300 mm diameter and up to 5 t on the rotator, with the boom covering 4000 mm vertically and horizontally. Vessel length is limited by the foundation and the crane, not by the cell.

Are the rails included?

No. Foundation rails and press blocks are laid by the customer to the drawing supplied, because they depend on the shop floor and the vessel sizes you will run.

Can it weld inside and outside seams?

Yes. The manipulator places the head at the seam on either side of the shell, and the rotator turns the vessel for circumferential seams and holds it for longitudinal ones.

How is the seam aligned?

Lift positions the boom at 1000 mm/min, stretch runs at 120 to 1000 mm/min, and the 100 x 100 mm cross slider trims the torch onto the joint.

What welding process is used?

Submerged arc welding with a 1000 A power source, wire feeder and flux recovery, which suits long shell seams where deposition and finish both matter.
