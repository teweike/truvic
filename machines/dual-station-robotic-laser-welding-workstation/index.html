Dual Station Robotic Laser Welding Workstation

Two stations, one robot: while the operator unloads and re-fixtures at station A, the robot welds at station B. That is the whole economy of the cell — the laser never waits for a human, and the human never waits for the laser. A 2 kW fiber source with switchable beam profiles (Gaussian, ring or hybrid) feeds a scan head that oscillates the beam in X and Y for stir, wobble or spiral patterns, and detects wire break so a bad feed stops the cycle instead of leaving an unwelded seam. Fixtures come from a 3D platform of grid holes and pins, so a new part is built from standard blocks rather than a machined jig.

Key Advantages

The four things that decide cell payback.

Two stations, no idle laser

Loading and unloading happen at one station while the robot welds the other. Laser-on time is limited by the weld, not by the operator — which is where the payback comes from on a 30-second part.

Beam you can shape

Gaussian, ring or hybrid profiles, with a pulsed center spot and a continuous-wave ring, switch per material. Wobble and spiral oscillation from the scan head widen the process window instead of forcing a perfect gap.

±0.05 mm over 2032 mm

A 12 kg payload robot at ±0.05 mm repeatability covers a two-metre working radius — enough for a 3500 mm station worked in two positions, without moving the part.

Fixtures from a grid, not a drawing

The 3D platform's grid holes and pins accept standard blocks, supports and quick clamps. A new part is fixtured in minutes without machining a dedicated jig, which is what makes small batches viable.

Wire break stops the cycle

The welding head detects wire break and the control stops, so a feed problem shows up as a stopped cycle rather than as a seam welded without filler.

Frequently Asked Questions

What buyers ask before committing to a two-station laser cell.

Does the robot really keep welding while we load?

Yes — that is the point of two stations. While one station is being unloaded and re-fixtured, the robot welds the other, so laser utilisation is set by the weld cycle rather than by handling.

What size parts fit?

A 2032 mm working radius and stations of 3500 × 800 mm. Parts are worked in two positions within the station, and the 12 kg payload covers fixtures plus the head.

Can it weld without filler wire?

The head supports wire feeding with wire-break detection, so both autogenous and filler-wire welds are possible. Which one applies depends on joint fit-up and material thickness.

What beam shapes are available?

Gaussian, ring and hybrid, switchable from the laser source, with the scan head adding stir, wobble or spiral oscillation. That combination lets you trade penetration against gap tolerance.

What power does the cell need?

Single-phase 220 V for the control cabinet, plus the laser and chiller supply per their nameplates. Cooling is water, through the dual-circuit chiller.

How long does a fixture change take?

Fixtures come from the grid-hole platform: blocks, supports and quick clamps are placed on the pins, no machining. A new part is typically fixtured while the other station runs.
