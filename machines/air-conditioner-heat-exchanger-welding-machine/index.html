Air Conditioner Heat Exchanger Welding Machine

A 15-metre through-line that brazes the return bends of air conditioner and refrigeration coils. Workpieces ride a VFD conveyor from the 5 m pre-brazing section through a 2 m flame station to an 8 m post-brazing run — no manual loading at the burner. The flame comes from mixed LPG and oxygen, the copper tube bore is protected by nitrogen, flux is carried in by the gas, and compressed-air nozzles cool the fins so they are not burnt through. One operator sets the recipe on a color touch screen and the line repeats it.

Key Advantages

Five decisions this line makes for you, in the order they show up on the shop floor.

15 m, one operator

5 m pre-brazing, 2 m brazing and 8 m post-brazing on a single roller-chain conveyor. Loading and unloading happen away from the flame, so one operator can run the line and inspect at the same time.

Speed you can dial in

0–4.5 m/min through a frequency converter, entered and read in m/min on the touch screen. Change coil size and you change the number, not the hardware.

Flame that stays put

The intelligent gas control system holds LPG and oxygen flow steady, so flame length does not drift across a shift — the usual cause of overheated or under-brazed return bends.

Nothing burns where it should not

Nitrogen protects the copper bore, flux carried by the gas cleans the joint, compressed-air nozzles keep the aluminum fins off the burn line, and the water-cooled fire row plus flow interlock protect the tooling.

Recipes, not guesswork

At least ten parameter sets stored in the color HMI: gas pressures, conveyor speed, flame timing. Call up a coil model, run it, and the next shift repeats the same result.

Frequently Asked Questions

The five points coil shops raise before signing — answered with the line data, not a brochure.

What joint does this line make?

It brazes the connection between the long U tube and the small U tube of a heat exchanger — the return bends on fin-and-tube coils. The workpiece is conveyed into the brazing zone, the fire row swings across the joint, and the part leaves on the same chain.

How is the copper tube protected from oxidation?

Nitrogen is fed into the copper tube bore at 0.01–0.10 MPa with an adjustable fill time, and the joint is brazed with flux carried in by the gas. After brazing, nitrogen purges the passage so residual flux cannot block it.

Will the aluminum fins get burnt?

Compressed-air nozzles on both sides of the weld zone cool the fins, the fire row itself is water-cooled, and the flame is set by recipe. The design target is fewer than six burnt pieces per workpiece, checked at commissioning.

What gases and pressures do I need on site?

LPG 0.08–0.12 MPa, oxygen 0.3–0.9 MPa, low-pressure nitrogen 0.4–0.6 MPa, compressed air 0.50–0.55 MPa and cooling water 0.4–0.6 MPa at the points of use. Anything outside these ranges needs a regulator added at installation.

Can the line run different coil models?

Yes. At least ten parameter sets are stored in the color touch screen; conveyor speed is entered in m/min and the gas panel holds the matching flame settings. Changing model is a recipe call, not a mechanical changeover.

What about safety with an open flame line?

Flashback arrestors sit on the LPG and oxygen lines inside and outside the gas cabinet, an exhaust fan keeps the cabinet free of combustible gas, the brazing zone has a heat shield with a top vent, and the cooling-water flow switch cuts the gas if circulation stops.
