Key Advantages

Why a flash butt weld instead of a braze — and what that changes downstream.

A metallurgical joint, not a filler joint

Flash butt welding upsets the two tube ends together under current and pressure. There is no flux residue inside the bore and no brazing alloy to reflow, so the joint is not sensitive to a later oven cure or a pressure cycle.

Same shape, same quality, every cycle

Secondary constant voltage and constant current control, a high-precision lathe-type lever and an air receiver holding upset pressure together keep the weld collar geometry repeatable from the first piece of the shift to the last.

Schedules stored by tube size

Different tube sizes get their own schedule number in the controller. The operator selects the size; the machine brings up the matching current and timing instead of re-setting the panel.

Clamping that does not leak

Aluminum-alloy cylinders with high-precision oil bearings give the ≥ 30 mm clamping stroke and 5–25 mm lateral adjustment without a gas-leak path at the clamp — one of the usual causes of scrap on this process.

The shrinker comes with the set

The shrinker rolls the copper end down (φ4–35 mm) so it inserts cleanly into the aluminum tube. Shrinking and welding are matched, so the fit-up that decides weld quality is not left to hand tools.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions that come up on Cu-Al transition joints.

Why weld copper to aluminum instead of brazing it?

A flash butt weld joins the two metals directly, so there is no brazing alloy or flux in the bore and the joint tolerates later heat cycles. It also removes the filler-metal cost and the operator skill that a good brace needs.

Do I need the shrinker as well?

Yes, for this joint family. The copper end is rolled down by the shrinker so it fits inside the aluminum tube; that fit-up is what makes the upset weld repeatable. The set is quoted with one mold per machine.

What tube sizes can it handle?

φ6.0–9.52 mm on both copper and aluminum for the welder; the tube shrinker covers φ4–35 mm. Other sizes need different clamp tooling — send us the OD and wall thickness.

What does a 50% duty cycle mean in production?

At 50% duty the machine is rated for alternating weld and cooling time in equal proportion. Give us your joint size and pieces per hour and we confirm whether that fits or a larger capacity is needed.

How is the weld quality kept repeatable?

Secondary constant voltage and constant current control, a high-precision lathe-type lever, and an air receiver holding the upset pressure. Schedule numbers stored per tube size keep the settings from drifting between operators.

Is cooling water mandatory?

The transformer is force water-cooled. You can run from a plant cooling circuit or add the optional low-temperature chiller; running without adequate water flow shortens electrode and transformer life.
