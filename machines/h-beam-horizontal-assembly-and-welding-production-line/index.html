H beam Horizontal Assembly and Welding Production Line

Instead of assembling a T-section and then welding it into an H, this line lays the beam on its side: web flat, flanges vertical, workpiece moving past the torch. Two seams weld at once in that position, which is why a horizontal line produces less deformation than a vertical one — the heat is balanced across the section instead of stacked. Tip the beam 180° and the other two seams go down the back welding machine. Assembly at the end is only a tack weld, not a full seam, so the line stays short: end assembly, front weld, turnover, back weld, conveyors. Four to five operators run the whole line.

Key Advantages

Why horizontal beats the classic vertical line on light beams.

Two seams per pass

With the web flat and flanges vertical, both seams are in position for one gantry pass. Two arcs on opposite sides balance the heat, so the section does not pull itself out of shape the way a single-side vertical weld does.

Assembly is a tack, not a seam

Only the end is spot welded before the beam goes to the welder. The machine then clamps and positions the three plates and lays the real seams — assembly time drops because the operator is not welding the whole joint twice.

Thin-wall friendly

Defined for webs 5–12 mm, this line is aimed at light-duty beams where a vertical line's deformation would need correction afterwards. The drift angle is held within 8°.

Four to five people

Assembly, two welding stations, turnover and conveying are laid out as one flow. The line crew stays at four to five, against the larger crew a three-station vertical layout needs.

Turnover instead of a second fixture

The turnover station flips the beam 180° in two steps and the conveyor feeds it straight into the back welder — no crane, no re-fixture, no second fit-up.

Frequently Asked Questions

What fabricators weigh before choosing a horizontal line.

Why horizontal rather than the usual vertical line?

Welding with the web flat puts both seams in position for one pass and balances the heat across the section, so deformation is lower. It also lets assembly be a tack weld at the end instead of a full seam, which shortens the line and the labor.

What sections does the line handle?

Web 300–1500 mm high and 5–12 mm thick, flange 150–500 mm wide and 5–20 mm thick, lengths 4000–15000 mm, up to 4000 kg per workpiece.

How fast is welding?

0.3–1.9 m/min. At 1.6 mm wire the typical setting is about 1.2 m/min twin-wire, which gives a sound seam with a clean appearance.

How many operators?

Four to five for the complete line — assembly, front welding, turnover, back welding and conveying as one flow.

Can I buy only part of the line?

Yes. A half line is assembling machine plus front welding machine, for shops that weld the remaining seams on another machine or by hand.

Do we need to prepare foundations?

Yes — each station needs a prepared foundation, and the buyer completes the machine foundations in advance of installation.
