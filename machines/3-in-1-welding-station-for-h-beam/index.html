3 in 1 Welding Station for H Beam

Fit-up, welding and straightening used to be three stations, three floor areas and three sets of handling. This machine does all three in one pass: the web and flange are hydraulically centered and clamped, a gantry press closes the gap between web and flange, a single-arc single-wire submerged arc torch welds the seam with flux recovered and re-fed, and hydraulic straightening rolls press the flange flat as the beam leaves the welding zone. One operator loads the section, the machine does the rest — which is how a beam shop gets the same output from a shorter line and fewer people.

Key Advantages

Why one station beats three.

Three operations, one pass

Fit-up, welding and straightening happen in sequence as the beam travels once through the machine. No crane move, no re-clamp, no queue between stations — the handling that normally eats the labor budget disappears.

Ground floor and people

One station replaces three machines, three foundations and the operators between them. That is floor area you can give to another product line, and labor you can move to fabrication.

Geometry clamped before the arc

Web and flange are hydraulically centered by screw leads and held by the gantry press, so the joint is closed when welding starts rather than relying on fit-up from the previous station.

Straightened on the way out

Hydraulic top rollers press the flange against chain-driven bottom rollers as the beam leaves the weld zone. Straightening follows welding in the same pass, so the beam is not re-handled after cooling.

Flux that comes back

Welding flux is recovered, cleaned and re-fed by the recovery unit, which keeps the seam covered and cuts flux consumption — the reason a submerged arc station can run continuously rather than in batches.

Frequently Asked Questions

What beam fabricators ask before ordering a combined station.

Can one machine really fit up, weld and straighten?

Yes. Centring, clamping, gantry pressing, submerged arc welding, flux recovery and straightening are sequenced in one pass through the station, with one operator at the control box.

What section sizes does it cover?

Web 150–1500 mm high and 5–25 mm thick, flange 150–800 mm wide and 6–30 mm thick, beam length 4000–15000 mm. Web plate from 14 mm welds soundly but is not fully penetrated — say so on the drawing if full penetration is required.

How fast does it weld?

350–1500 mm/min, set by the wire diameter and the procedure. 3.2 and 4.0 mm wire are both supported by the 70 kVA power sources.

What about H beam distortion?

The gantry press closes the web-flange gap before the arc starts and the straightening rollers correct the flange as the beam leaves the weld zone, in the same pass rather than after cooling.

Can it make T-beams too?

Yes — the same clamping, welding and straightening setup builds T-sections when only one flange is welded.

What do I need on site?

380 V 3-phase supply for about 2 × 70 kVA welding plus motors, dry compressed air for the flux circuit, welding wire, and floor length for the longest beam plus conveyor run-in and run-out.
