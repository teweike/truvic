Key Advantages

Why flatten and punch at one station.

One set-up, two operations

Flattening and punching run in sequence on the same press, so the valve hole is cut into a face that has just been pressed flat instead of a face that has sprung back.

The table meets the rim profile

The 30 degree tilt lets the rim face sit square under the ram, which is what makes the pressed face flat rather than pressed at an angle.

Punch force sized for the hole

160 kN on a 100 mm punch stroke is sized for a valve hole, so the punch is not asked to do the flattening work.

Deep throat, open front

150 mm throat depth reaches the valve hole position on the rim face, with the rim loaded by hand from the front.

Slow pressing speed

4–10 mm/s pressing speed keeps the face from springing back and keeps the hole clean.

Last station on the line

It takes the expanded rim and delivers the finished face and valve hole, so the rim leaves the line complete.

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions buyers ask about flattening and valve hole punching.

Why flatten before punching?

Because a hole cut into a face that has just been pressed flat stays round and square. Punching first and flattening after would distort the hole.

Why does the table tilt?

So the rim face sits square under the ram. On a profiled rim face a level table would press at an angle and leave the face convex.

Can it punch different hole sizes?

Yes. The punch and die are changed for the valve hole size; the stroke stays at 100 mm.

What is the maximum opening?

600 mm from the slide to the table, with a 300 mm slide stroke.

Is it automatic?

It runs in automatic mode once set, with manual and setting modes for a profile change, plus over-stroke and over-pressure protection.
