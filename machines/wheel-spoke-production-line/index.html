Wheel Spoke Production Line

A complete stamping line for passenger-car wheel spokes, from coil or strip to a finished disc. Six stations run in sequence: blanking, first draw, forming, bolt-hole punching, vent-hole punching and vent-hole extrusion.

The main forming stations use four-column hydraulic presses of 5,000, 6,300 and 8,000 kN, while the punching and extrusion stations use 1,000-1,600 kN open mechanical presses. The line is delivered as a matched set, with press capacities, die heights and pass heights selected together so the dies and transfers fit.

Key Advantages

Why the line is bought as a set rather than as individual presses.

Press sizes matched to the process

Each station is sized for its own operation: 8,000 kN for forming, 5,000 kN for draw and bolt holes, 1,600 kN and below for vent holes.

One pass height for the whole line

Die heights, daylight and conveyor height are set together, so dies transfer between stations without re-setting the line.

Fast punching stations

Open presses at 45 strokes per minute keep the punching stations ahead of the forming cycle.

Die and operator protection

Mis-feed detection, hydraulic overload, two-hand start and guards are standard on every press.

Serviceable die space

Open press throat of 405 mm and 680 mm column gap give room to change dies and reach the slide.

Delivered as a working set

Presses, controls, conveyors and die-height data are quoted together, so the line is commissioned as one route.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions buyers ask before ordering a spoke stamping line.

Can the line use my existing dies?

Yes, if the die heights and shut heights fall within the ranges listed. Send the die drawings and we will confirm which stations need adaptor plates.

Is the 8,000 kN press needed for all spokes?

No. For smaller or thinner spokes the forming station can be quoted at 6,300 kN; the 8,000 kN size is for larger discs and higher-strength steel.

How many operators does the line need?

Two to three per shift: one at the blanking and draw end, one at the punching stations, one for inspection and packing.

Do you supply the dies?

Dies are usually customer-supplied or built locally. We quote the presses around your die set and can introduce a die supplier if needed.

What is the lead time?

Presses are built to order; the line is shipped as a set so the stations arrive together for a single commissioning.
