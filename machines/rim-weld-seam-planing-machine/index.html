Rim Weld Seam Planing Machine

A C-frame machine that planes the flash off the rim seam after flash butt welding. Six blades — three above the seam, three below — run on a hydraulically driven slide while the rim is held by a hydraulic clamp, leaving the seam 0.2 mm above the parent metal so the following rolling pass can bring it flush. Strip 3 to 6 mm thick, 10 to 15 seconds a piece.

Key Advantages

Why planing, not grinding, before the rolling pass.

A cut, not a grind

The blades take the flash off in a controlled chip. There is no heat-affected zone and no abrasive dust on the rim surface.

Six blades in a stepped set

Three blades per face stepped 0.2–0.3 mm each share the cut, which keeps the load per blade low and the seam even along its length.

Linked carrier and clamp

The blade carrier and the clamp are tied by one mechanism, so the blade-to-rim position stays fixed and a thickness change needs no resetting.

Held still while it is cut

The hydraulic clamp with its stop block holds the rim against axial movement, which is what keeps the seam square to the blade.

PLC with manual and auto

The cycle is set up in manual mode and run on two-hand start in automatic, with proximity switches confirming both ends of the stroke.

One hydraulic station for the line

The planing, rolling and end-cutting stations share a hydraulic and electrical system, which keeps the line footprint and the maintenance points down.

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions buyers ask about weld flash removal.

Why leave 0.2 mm above the parent metal?

Because the rolling pass that follows needs material to roll. Planing the seam flush would leave the roll nothing to work with; 0.2 mm is the allowance the roller is set to.

Does the blade setting change with strip thickness?

No. The blade carrier and the clamp are linked, so the machine follows the material thickness without a reset. The blade step is set once for the seam profile.

How often are blades changed?

Blades are ground and reused; the interval depends on the seam height and the strip grade. Each blade carrier is set so the last blade finishes the cut.

Is it automatic?

Yes in automatic mode, started with two hands for safety. Manual mode is used for setting the blades and checking the cycle.

Does it need compressed air?

No. The planing cycle is hydraulic; air is only needed elsewhere on the line.
