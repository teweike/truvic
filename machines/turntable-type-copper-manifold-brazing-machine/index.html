Turntable-Type Copper Manifold Brazing Machine

For mass production of copper manifolds, flame brazing is the fast route — and this machine turns it into a rotary process. Fixtures ride a graduated turntable past heating, brazing and cooling stations; the operator only loads and unloads. Two flame brazing systems with two automatic wire feeders work the joints from opposite sides, torches adjust in every direction for different manifold sizes, and gas pressure, flow and heating time are preset in the controller instead of set by hand. Stainless fixtures hold the parts without deforming at brazing temperature, and stations detect whether a part is present before they fire.

Key Advantages

What a rotary brazing layout buys you on a manifold line.

Load and unload only

Fixtures index through heat, braze and cool on their own. The operator's job shrinks to placing and removing parts, which is the only way a flame process reaches a 30-second cycle without a second person at the torch.

Two torches, two feeders

Two flame systems with two automatic wire feeders braze from both sides of the joint. Fill is fed, not dipped, so the amount of alloy per joint stays the same part to part.

30 seconds, repeatable

Cycle time is set by the table index and the preset heating time, not by how fast an operator can judge the flame color. The same recipe runs the same manifold all shift.

Stainless fixtures, nitrogen inside

Stainless fixtures hold the part at brazing temperature without deforming, and nitrogen purge protects the bore so the manifold does not scale up internally.

Stations that know a part is there

Heating and cooling stations detect the part before they fire, and the safety chain cuts gas automatically — which is what makes an unattended rotary flame process acceptable in a production shop.

Frequently Asked Questions

The points manifold shops raise before ordering a rotary brazing machine.

What parts can this machine braze?

Copper and brass tube assemblies — copper manifolds, distributor headers and similar parts where several branch tubes join one body. Send the drawing and we confirm fixture layout and whether one index cycle covers all joints.

How fast is it really?

One part per 30-second cycle on the quoted configuration. That figure assumes the joints fall within the torch swing range of 5–10 mm and the 175 mm torch stretch stroke; a taller manifold may need a second heating position.

Do operators need brazing skills?

No flame judgement is required. Gas pressure, flow and heating time are preset in the controller and recalled per part number, and the stations fire only when a part is detected. Loading and unloading are the manual steps.

How is the bore protected from oxidation?

Nitrogen purge through the joint during brazing, plus preset heating time so the part is not overheated. That keeps the internal passage clean without a post-braze pickling step.

What gases do I need on site?

LPG or CNG at 0.065–0.12 MPa and compressed air at 0.5–0.55 MPa at the points of use, plus cooling water and an exhaust duct from the hood.

Is a fixture change expensive?

Fixtures are stainless steel sets, one per manifold family. Changing family is a fixture swap on the table, not a machine rebuild — tell us the families you run and we quote the sets with the machine.
