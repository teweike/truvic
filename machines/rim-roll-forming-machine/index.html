Key Advantages

Why rim plants buy this station instead of a general press.

Two passes, one setup

The first pass rough-forms the blank, the second finishes the profile. Both runs use the same shafts and the same mold, so size changes are a mold swap, not a machine rebuild.

Speed-matched rolls

Each shaft has its own hydraulic motor and throttle. Setting the two linear speeds equal is what keeps the rim surface and the mold free of score marks.

Programmed infeed

Fast approach, slow feed, dwell and retract run off a stop plate and proximity switch, so every rim sees the same rolling time and the same force.

Rigid spindle support

The upper bearing housing is part of the frame and the front bearing is a three-row cylindrical roller bearing — the shaft does not deflect under 230 kN.

Built to sit in a line

Loading arms, guide wheels and the height of the working center are set so the machine drops between the flash butt welder and the finishing stations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions we get from rim plant engineers.

How many passes does a rim need?

Two. The first pass rough-forms the blank and centers it with edge wheels; the second finishes the bead seat and flange with entry and exit guide wheels.

Can it form a different rim size without new shafts?

Yes — the shaft center distance moves between 262 and 420 mm and the mold is swapped. The 13 to 18 inch range is covered by mold sets, not by changing spindles.

What decides the cycle time?

The dwell at full force. Ten to fifteen seconds covers fast approach, slow feed, the dwell roll and retract, and it is set on the stop plate.

What material can it roll?

Steel with 540 to 650 MPa tensile and at least 450 MPa yield — the grades used for wheel rims.
