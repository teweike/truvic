Rim Roll-Marking, Ring-Forming and Flattening Machine

A horizontal five-roll machine that does three jobs in one pass: a marking roll prints the rim code on the strip as it enters, three driven forming rolls roll the strip into a closed ring, and a four-cylinder flattening unit at the outlet presses that ring flat. Strip from 980 to 1500 mm long, 150 to 400 mm wide and 2 to 5 mm thick, at 6 to 15 seconds a piece.

Key Advantages

Three operations, one pass, one operator.

The mark is rolled on while the strip moves

The marking roll sits ahead of the feed rolls and swings onto the travelling strip, so the code is rolled in step with the material and there is no separate marking station.

Five rolls, one frame

Three forming rolls and two feed rolls are driven together, so the ring is formed and fed by the same roll train rather than by two machines in series.

Digital readout on the opening

The ring opening and the helix are set by handwheel and shown on a digital display, which makes a size change a repeatable setting instead of trial and error.

Anti-double-feed device

A mechanical device keeps a second blank out of the pass, which protects the rolls and keeps the cycle time honest.

Flattened before it leaves

The four-cylinder unit on the outlet closes and flattens the ring in the same machine, so the ring does not need a second handling step.

Fed from the loading rack

The machine takes its blanks from the automatic loading rack and hands the closed ring to the welder, which is how the line runs as one unit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions buyers ask about a combined marking and forming machine.

Does marking slow the cycle down?

No. The marking roll swings onto the strip ahead of the feed rolls and rolls the mark on while the strip is moving, so marking is inside the forming cycle.

What strip sizes can it run?

980 to 1500 mm long, 150 to 400 mm wide, 2 to 5 mm thick. At 5 mm the width is limited to 350 mm.

How is the ring size changed?

By the handwheel on the opening and the helix, with a digital readout of the setting. The roll tooling stays in the machine.

Is the flattening a separate station?

No. It is mounted on the outlet of the same machine with its own hydraulic station, so the ring is flattened before it leaves.

What does it need to run?

380 V three-phase power, 0.6 MPa air at 100 L/min or more, a level floor and 200 L of hydraulic oil.
