Key Advantages

Why workshops replace three separate machines with this one.

Four operations, one footprint

Punching, shearing, horizontal bending and vertical bending sit on one 1.7 m machine, so a bar travels between stations by hand instead of between machines.

Turret punching

Eight positions on the turret and four heads fitted as standard. Changing hole size is an index, not a die change, which is what keeps small batches profitable.

Clean shear, no scrap

The double-column shear cuts square with no burr and no offcut, so busbar length is taken from the drawing rather than from a saw allowance.

Repeatable angles

Bending angle is set on the PLC and stored per program, so the second and hundredth bar come out at the same angle to within one degree.

Built for copper and aluminum

Dies and blades are matched to copper and aluminum bar up to 12 mm, including the softer aluminum grades that mark easily.

Frequently Asked Questions

What switchgear workshops ask before ordering.

Which operations can one machine do?

Punching, shearing, horizontal bending, vertical bending, embossing and tie-in pressing. Twisting and flattening are done with the same tooling set on request.

How thick a bar can it process?

12 mm on all three main stations, and 10 mm for vertical bending at 120 mm width.

Do I have to change dies to change the hole size?

No. The turret carries eight positions and four heads are fitted as standard, so hole size changes by indexing the turret.

Can two operators work at the same time?

Yes. The bending station has two separate tables and each station has its own foot switch, so punching and bending can run in parallel.
