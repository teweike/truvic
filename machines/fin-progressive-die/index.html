Fin Progressive Die

The die is where fin quality is decided. This progressive die stamps the fin profile, the tube holes and the collars in one pass at 150 to 220 strokes per minute, with high-speed steel punches, slitting knives, cross-cutting knives and window knives. It is built for a 450 kN fin press, comes with its mounting accessories and a spare insert set, and is designed for 30 to 35 million strokes with at least ten regrinds over its life.

Key Advantages

Why a die specification matters as much as the press behind it.

No hole sinking, no cracking

Fin quality is a die question. The stations are set so fins come out flat with clear profiles and no cracks at the secondary flanging.

150–220 SPM without chatter

High-speed steel edges and a rigid block hold the clearance at production speed, which is where cheaper tooling starts to mark the fin.

30–35 million strokes

The die is planned for a full production life with at least ten regrinds, not for a trial run.

Spares in the first shipment

Springs, punches, dies, sleeves and slitting blades come with the die, so a worn insert does not stop the line for a week.

Built to your fin drawing

Hole pattern, fin pitch and flanging heights are all cut to your drawing, so the die matches the coil you already sell.

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions buyers ask before a fin die is ordered.

Will it fit my existing press?

The die is made to a 450 kN fin press with 210 to 270 mm die height and a 660 x 850 mm table. Send the press shut height and table drawing and the mounting is matched to it.

How often do I regrind?

Remove 0.05 to 0.1 mm per grinding operation, and plan at least ten regrinds across the 30 to 35 million stroke life. The interval itself depends on foil quality and lubrication.

Do I get spare cutting edges?

Yes. Springs, piercing punches and dies, sleeves and upper and lower slitting blades are supplied in pairs with the die.

Can the die run a second fin profile?

Only with a second die block. Fin pitch and hole pattern are cut into the block, so a different fin is a different die, not an insert change.

What foil quality does it expect?

Clean, evenly oiled foil. Contamination and dry spots are the usual cause of punch wear, which is why the line uses an oil-through device with a leveller.
