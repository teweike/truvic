Extruded Fin Tube Machine

A cold-extrusion finning machine. An aluminium sleeve is drawn over the base tube and rolled onto it through the forming head, so the fin is extruded out of the sleeve in one pass and stays integral with the tube wall — base tube 10 to 50 mm OD, fins 3 to 15 mm high at 5 to 15 fins per inch.

Key Advantages

Why extruded fins hold up where welded and embedded fins do not.

Integral fin, no joint to fail

The fin is extruded out of the aluminium sleeve, so fin and sleeve are one piece. There is no weld line or embedded foot to crack under thermal cycling.

Better contact, better heat transfer

The rolled fin sits tight on the base tube along its whole length, which keeps the contact resistance low and the heat transfer predictable.

Compact machine, stable output

A bench-type frame with a geared drive: compact footprint, stable running and simple operation, which is what small and mid-size fin tube shops ask for.

One pass, no second operation

Tube and sleeve go through the head once and come out finned. No brazing, no winding, no second pass to close the fin.

Range from 10 to 50 mm tube

The same machine covers base tube from 10 to 50 mm OD, fin height 3 to 15 mm and 5 to 15 fins per inch, with tooling per specification.

Tooling matched to the fin

Rolls and cutter are built to the fin drawing: height, pitch and thickness are set by tooling, so a new fin is a tooling change, not a new machine.

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions buyers ask before a finning machine is ordered.

How is an extruded fin different from a wound fin?

A wound fin is a strip wrapped and usually tension-wound onto the tube; an extruded fin is rolled out of an aluminium sleeve that has been drawn over the base tube, so the fin is integral with the sleeve and there is no joint at the foot.

Which base tube materials can it run?

Copper, carbon steel and stainless steel, from 10 to 50 mm OD. The base tube is the pressure boundary; the aluminium sleeve is what becomes the fin.

What fin sizes are possible?

Fin height 3 to 15 mm, density 5 to 15 fins per inch, thickness 0.3 to 1.2 mm, each set by the tooling in the head. The fin specification is what the tooling is built to.

How long can the tube be?

1 to 12 m as standard, with entry and exit supports. Longer lengths are quoted with the support arrangement.

Does it need compressed air or a second machine?

No. The fin is formed in one pass through the head; the machine needs only a power supply and a level floor.

What decides the output rate?

Fin pitch, fin height, tube size and the speed set on the panel. The tooling sets the fin; the speed sets how fast the tube runs through the head.

What is included with the machine?

The machine with the finning head and guards, the roll set and cutter for the ordered fin, tube supports, the control panel, the English manual and on-site commissioning with training.
