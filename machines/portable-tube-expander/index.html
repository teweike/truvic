Portable Tube Expander

When a coil has to be expanded on site or in small batches, a fixed expander is the wrong tool. This portable unit clamps with an air cylinder driven petal jaw, drives the expansion head on a gear rack and expands four or eight tubes per cycle, with the torque range 171 to 219 N.m set by the tube specification. The head hangs from a 2 m gantry with a balancer, so one operator can move from tube to tube without lifting it.

Key Advantages

What a portable expander does that a fixed machine cannot.

Take it to the coil

A coil too large to move, or a repair on a customer's site, is exactly what this unit is for. The head hangs from a balancer so weight is not the operator's problem.

Clamped by air, not by hand

The petal jaw is driven by an air cylinder, so the tube is held identically on every pass and the torque goes into the expansion instead of into fighting a vice.

Four or eight tubes at a time

Multiple heads per cycle turn a repair job into a batch job, and the rack rods keep each head aligned with its tube.

Torque set to the tube

The 171 to 219 N·m range covers thin-wall copper through stainless, with the setting confirmed against the tube specification.

Limits and grounding

Front and rear travel limits, full grounding and an emergency stop are standard, which matters when the unit works beside an assembled core.

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions buyers ask before a portable expander is ordered.

Why portable instead of a vertical expander?

A vertical machine expands a whole core in one stroke and needs the core brought to it. This unit goes to the coil, which suits service calls, repairs and small batches.

How many tubes per cycle?

Four or eight, depending on how many heads are fitted. The rack expansion rods keep each head on its tube.

What torque do I need?

It depends on tube material and wall. The unit covers 171 to 219 N·m, and the setting is confirmed against your tube specification rather than guessed.

Does it flare as well?

A tube flaring gun is supplied for each specification, so the end is flared with the same unit after expansion.

Can it expand stainless?

Yes. Stainless steel and copper are both listed as applicable material, with the head and torque set for the tube in question.
